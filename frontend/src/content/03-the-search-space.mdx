Three of the four methods here search the same space, and it is about as small as a search space
gets: **which of the twelve encoder layers to keep**. Twelve bits, with the constraint that at least
four stay on.

Nothing else varies. Hidden size stays at 768, attention heads stay at twelve, the feed-forward
block stays at 3072 wide, and the tokenizer and embeddings are untouched. The AlphaNAS report says
head count and FFN width were considered and dropped as having little influence on the task; whether
that was the right call is arguable, but it does make the space small enough to reason about
completely.

## The cost function is a straight line

Because the only thing that varies is how many layers survive, and every layer is identical in size,
the parameter count is exact arithmetic:

```
params(k) = 24 429 314 + 7 087 872 · k
```

24 429 314 is everything outside the encoder stack - token, position and segment embeddings, their
LayerNorm, the pooler and the two-way classifier - and it does not move. 7 087 872 is one encoder
layer. Twelve layers give 109 483 778, which is what a loaded checkpoint reports, to the parameter.

Two things are worth noticing while clicking.

**The floor is high.** Drop every layer and 24.4 million parameters remain, because the embedding
table alone is 23.4 million of them. Layer selection can get you to 48% of the original model; it
cannot get you to 10%. That is the ceiling on this entire family of methods, visible before any
training happens, and it is why the one method that does reach 7.8 million had to throw the
transformer away rather than trim it.

**FLOPs and parameters are not the same axis.** Parameters are linear in the layer count. FLOPs are
linear in the layer count _and_ quadratic in sequence length, because attention compares every token
to every other one. Move the sequence-length control and the arithmetic cost changes by a factor the
parameter count never sees. A model can be half the size and nowhere near half the cost, depending
entirely on how long the inputs are.

## What this space cannot tell you

The mask fixes the cost completely and says nothing about the quality. Two four-layer masks have
identical parameter counts, identical FLOPs, identical footprints - and there is no reason to expect
identical accuracy, because layers at different depths have learned different things.

Finding out which four-layer mask is the good one is the entire job of the search. It costs a
training run per candidate, which is where the next four chapters go.
