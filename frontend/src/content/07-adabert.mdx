The first three methods pick layers out of a pretrained BERT. AdaBERT does something different in
kind: it builds a small network from scratch and teaches it to imitate the big one.

## Two ideas at once

**Differentiable architecture search.** Choosing between seven candidate operations is a discrete
decision, and you cannot differentiate through a discrete decision. So you relax it: give each
operation a weight, take a weighted mixture of all of them, and learn the weights by gradient descent
along with everything else. Gumbel-softmax makes the mixture behave like a hard choice while staying
differentiable, with a temperature controlling how sharply it commits. Anneal the temperature and the
mixture collapses onto one operation per slot.

**Task-adaptive distillation.** The small network is trained to match the teacher's internal hidden
states, not just its final answer, with student layer _i_ mapped to teacher layer 3*i*. The task
supervises it too, but most of the signal comes from imitation.

The operations searched over are convolutions of width 1, 3 and 5, a dilated convolution, max
pooling, average pooling and a skip connection. Note what is absent: there is no attention anywhere
in the student. Hidden size is 256 against the teacher's 768.

The objective is a weighted sum of three terms:

```
L = 0.2 * cross_entropy + 0.8 * distillation + 1.5 * efficiency
```

## What it found

Every one of the five surviving cells selected `avg_pool`.

Average pooling has no parameters. Neither does max pooling or a skip connection, and the efficiency
term - weighted 1.5, the largest of the three - is a direct penalty on parameters and FLOPs. With
that weighting, the cheapest way to reduce the loss is to select operations that cost nothing, and
the search took it.

The resulting network is:

```
Embedding(30522, 256) -> mean over tokens -> Linear(256, 2)
```

which is 30522·256 + 256·2 + 2 = **7 814 146 parameters**. The checkpoint on the Hub is 31 258 949
bytes, exactly that many float32 values plus a header. The arithmetic and the artifact agree: there
is nothing else in the model.

## Calling it what it is

This is a bag-of-embeddings classifier - average the word vectors, run a linear layer. It is a
respectable baseline with a long history, it reaches 88% on this task, and it is genuinely the
fastest thing in the comparison by a wide margin. It is also not a compressed transformer, and a
result that reads "differentiable NAS produced a 14× smaller model at 88%" describes something other
than what happened.

The notebook's own diagnosis is that fifteen search epochs is too few where the literature uses fifty
to ninety, and that is consistent with what the training log shows: the loss sits at about 12.1
throughout, dominated by the efficiency term, and barely moves. With more epochs and a smaller
efficiency weight the search might well have kept the convolutions. Nobody has run it to find out,
which is the honest place to leave it.

## One more thing to be careful about

`FrozenAdaBERT` is defined twice in this repository's notebooks, and the two differ: the version in
`dnas/` stacks the five average-pooling cells, the version redefined in `results/` goes straight from
the embedding to the mean. Since average pooling has no parameters, `load_state_dict` succeeds either
way and neither errors - but the forward passes are not the same, which is the most likely reason the
same checkpoint scores 0.8979 in one notebook and 0.8801 in the other. This app loads the artifact
with `strict=True` so that the checkpoint decides which definition is correct, rather than the loader
guessing.
